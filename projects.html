03 / MY APPROACH

From a question to a clear chart.

Data science starts with asking a useful question. In a transaction dataset, for instance, I might ask which categories appear most often or whether spending amounts vary across customers. The question helps determine what information is needed and which visualizations could be useful. I am learning not to choose a chart simply because it looks interesting; I want the chart to answer something that matters.

After deciding what to investigate, I check the dataset. That can include looking for empty entries, duplicate records, inconsistent labels and columns stored in unexpected formats. Data cleaning helps make later comparisons more reliable. Then I use exploratory data analysis to summarize values and look for patterns, differences or unexpected observations. I have been practicing this process in Python and experimenting with Matplotlib charts to communicate what I find.

The next step is presentation. A useful chart should have clear labels and enough explanation that someone unfamiliar with the dataset can understand it. I also think it is important to explain limitations rather than make a stronger claim than the data supports. The D3 example above is deliberately labeled as sample data because it demonstrates a visualization technique, not a research result. As I continue learning, I want to build projects that combine careful analysis with simple explanations that other people can follow.

My analysis checklist

  • Start with a clear question.
  • Examine and clean the dataset.
  • Explore values and patterns.
  • Choose an appropriate chart.
  • Explain findings and limitations.
